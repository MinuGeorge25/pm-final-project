## Friction points

_The biggest technical or strategic challenge you faced while developing your product concept._

The hardest challenge was connecting the pain I had found to a number that could prove it. The interviews and tickets gave me a clear moment of misery for Lena: the card balance and her pay later orders on two screens, and two reminders for different amounts in the same week (N-07, T-103, T-104). The data pack measured usage, not pain. Month-2 activity falls from 77.6% to 51.0% for the Month 1 cohort, but that could be novelty fading (N-12) as easily as tracking friction. The test group was also mostly employees (253 of 431 activated cards), and my persona was a composite of different participants. So I could name the problem but not prove its size.

The second challenge was scope. My M3 hypothesis bundled three fixes: one balance, one due date and a failure reason. The feature I scoped, B3, delivers only the first, so the test I could actually run was narrower than the idea I believed in.

## Key learnings

_A few surprising discoveries or insights you gained from the course overall._

- **Plausible is not evidence.** "Cash-flow control" and "Merchant offers" sounded like sensible features, and an AI-written persona arrived with traits like "terminal panic" that no participant said. Each one fell apart when I asked which quote or number supported it.
- **AI drafts fast and sounds equally confident about guesses.** It was most useful when I gave it a structure and pushed back, and least reliable when it filled in numbers on its own.
- **Prioritisation depends on the metric you choose.** I lowered B4's value once I saw that nothing linked it to month-2 activity. The decline fix (B1) moved up when the data showed about half of declines were online, not in store.
- **Building exposes the logic of a plan.** The prototype showed that my PRD's "one tap to open" rule contradicted Lena's complaint about having one more thing to check, so I moved the view onto the home page.
- **Most of my numbers were assumptions.** The 62% target, the 58.8% guardrail floor, the 150-card cohort minimum and every effort score were mine, not the data pack's. Labelling them kept the plan honest.

## Aha! moment

_Your main "aha" moment during the project process._

My aha came when I pressure-tested my own experiment brief. I had written an 8-point goal and a clean decision rule, then checked whether the data could ever deliver it. It could not. At the pilot's pace of about 108 activations a month, reaching 597 cards per arm would take about 11 months of enrolment. My kill rule, a lift under +2 points, would also have rejected a real +4 point win about a quarter of the time.

A decision rule has to survive its own sample size. I rewrote it with a void check, an explicit inconclusive outcome, a kill line based on the confidence interval and a named owner, all committed before any data arrives.
