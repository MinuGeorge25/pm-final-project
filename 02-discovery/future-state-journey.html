Lena's future-state journey with Riverty's first card

Strategy: one card, one balance, one due date, and a clear answer whenever a payment fails. Friends-and-family MVP for Germany.

Who: Lena, 32, Cologne, heavy BNPL user Goal: pay only after deciding to keep an item, and see what she owes and when Friction: two screens, two reminders, "one more thing to check before payday"

How we will know it is working

Success metric: month-2 activity

Share of cards active two months after activation (cohort_activity.csv). The 62% target is an assumption.

Guardrail: partner merchant share of volume

Dashed line: floor of 58.8%, an assumption set at the Month 3 low (monthly_trend.csv).

Decision window

Go or no-go after two cohorts of at least 150 activated cards each, observed through month 2.

Go if both reach 62% or more and the guardrail holds. Rethink if both stay at 51 to 58%, or if either falls below 51%. The 150-card minimum is an assumption.

Limits to state when presenting this map:

  • The data pack is fictional practice data, and Lena is a composite of quotes from different participants.
  • The emotion curve is illustrative. The manual workaround is inferred, since the research does not document spreadsheets, screenshots or calendar reminders.
  • The fixes are design intentions (B1, B2, B3, B8). The activity decay could be novelty fading as easily as tracking friction.
  • Not covered: late purchase notifications (T-108), the repeated ID photo request (T-106) and the in-store pay now or later choice (B4), which needs its own approval.
  • Employees hold 253 of the 431 activated cards, so real customers may behave differently.
